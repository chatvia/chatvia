Pinewood Outdoor: for businesses, schools and clubs, and about the company

ABOUT THIS DOCUMENT AND THE BUSINESS TEAM

This document is for companies, schools, youth groups and clubs that buy or borrow gear from us, and for anyone who wants to know more about Pinewood Outdoor as a company. It explains business accounts, gear loans, club discounts, printed clothing, sponsoring, our sustainability report, jobs, press questions, the services in our shops, and how we deal with complaints and data requests. The terms of sale still apply to every order. This text adds the details that business and group customers often ask us about. Last checked: 15 September 2026.

Our business team has four people and sits in the office at 20 Mill Lane. They open business accounts, make offers for large orders, plan printed clothing, and handle gear loans for schools. The business team does not work in the shops, so a shop cannot give you a business price at the till unless you have an account number.

You can reach the business team by email at business@pinewood.example, or by phone on +31 20 555 0140. The phone line is open Monday to Thursday from 8:30 to 16:30, and on Friday from 8:30 to 12:30. Outside these hours, leave a message and we call you back on the next working day.

When you ask for an offer, please tell us what you need, how many pieces, the sizes if you know them, and the date you need the gear by. We send most offers within three working days. An offer stays valid for 30 days, because we set our prices for the next season twice a year, in January and in July. If you accept an offer after 30 days, we check the prices again before we confirm it.

OPENING A BUSINESS ACCOUNT

Any registered company, school, public body, charity or sports club can open a business account. Private people cannot, even when they buy a lot from us. The account itself is free, and there is no yearly fee.

How to open an account:

Step 1: Fill in the business account form on pinewood.example/business. You need the legal name of your organisation, its registration number at the chamber of commerce (or the school number), and your VAT number if you have one.

Step 2: Name at least one buyer. A buyer is a person who may place orders for your organisation. You can name up to ten buyers, and each of them gets a personal login to the business portal.

Step 3: Upload a signed copy of our business terms. The person who signs must be allowed to sign for the organisation, such as a director, a head teacher or the chair of the club.

Step 4: Wait for our check. We check the details within three working days and then send you an account number that starts with PB, for example PB-2207.

With the account number, your buyers can order online at business prices, or give the number at the till in any of our shops in Europe. Every order on a business account gets an invoice with the name and VAT number of your organisation, so you can claim the VAT back where the law allows it.

If something changes, such as a new address or a buyer who leaves, update it in the business portal or tell the business team. We close a business account when nobody has used it for 24 months, but we always write to you before we do.

CREDIT LIMIT AND PAYMENT TERMS

Every new business account starts with a credit limit of 2,500. The credit limit is the most your organisation can owe us at one time on unpaid invoices. When an order would take you over the limit, you can still place it, but you pay the part above the limit by card or bank transfer before we send the order.

After six months without a late payment, you can ask for a higher limit. We look at how much you ordered and how quickly you paid, and we can raise the limit up to 10,000. Schools and public bodies can ask for up to 15,000 from the start, because their budgets are fixed for the whole year. A credit limit above 15,000 needs a bank guarantee.

As the terms of sale say, each invoice is due 30 days after delivery. We send invoices by email as a PDF. Public bodies that need it can get an e-invoice through the Peppol network instead. Please put the invoice number on your payment, so our accounts team can match it.

What happens when an invoice is not paid on time:

After 7 days: we send a friendly reminder by email.

After 21 days: we call the contact person of the account and charge a reminder fee of 15.

After 45 days: we stop all new orders on account until everything is paid, and we may add the legal interest for late payment.

If you already know that a payment will be late, for example because a school budget only arrives with the new term, tell us before the due date. We can then agree on a later date without any fee. Paying early does not give an extra discount, but it helps your credit limit grow faster.

VOLUME DISCOUNTS FOR BUSINESS CUSTOMERS

Business customers get a discount on large orders. The discount depends on the value of one order before VAT, and we take it off the invoice straight away. There are four tiers:

Tier 1: from 2,000 in one order, 5% off.

Tier 2: from 5,000 in one order, 8% off.

Tier 3: from 12,000 in one order, 11% off.

Tier 4: from 25,000 in one order, a price we agree with you, usually between 12% and 15% off.

There is also a yearly bonus. If your organisation spends more than 20,000 on its business account in one calendar year, we give you 3% of that amount as a credit note in January. The bonus is never paid out in money, and it is lost if the account is closed before January.

Some things never count towards a tier and never get a business discount: gift cards, gas cartridges, rentals, repairs, guided trips and Second Trail items. Logo printing and embroidery count towards the order value, but there is no discount on the setup fee.

A business discount cannot be added to a club discount, a student discount or a sale price. When more than one discount is possible, the till or the online store uses the one that is better for you. For example, during the summer sale a sale price of 30% off beats the 5% of tier 1, so you pay the sale price.

Please do not split one large order into several small ones, and do not join the orders of different organisations to reach a higher tier. Orders for several schools in one school group are fine, as long as the same account pays the invoice. If we find split or joined orders, we charge the difference to the correct tier on the next invoice.

GEAR LOANS FOR SCHOOLS AND YOUTH GROUPS

Through the School Gear Library we lend tents, sleeping bags, sleeping mats and backpacks to schools and youth groups for free. The idea is simple: no child should miss a school camp because the family cannot buy gear. The library began in 2017 with 30 tents, and today it holds about 400 items. Many of them come from Second Trail.

Who can borrow: primary and secondary schools; registered youth groups, such as scouts, guides and youth clubs; and charities that run camps for children or young people. A loan is for children and young people up to the age of 18. The group leader must be an adult who works or volunteers for the school or group, and that person signs for the loan.

What you can borrow in one loan: up to 15 tents, 40 sleeping bags, 40 sleeping mats and 20 backpacks, for at most 10 days. Each group can borrow twice in one school year. Stoves and cooking sets are not part of the library, because we do not lend gear that burns gas to groups of children.

How to book a loan:

Step 1: Ask for a loan with the form on pinewood.example/schools, at least six weeks before the trip.

Step 2: We check what is free on your dates and send a loan offer within five working days.

Step 3: The group leader signs the loan agreement and sends it back within one week. If we do not get it in time, the booking lapses and the gear goes to the next group.

From May to September many groups want to borrow, so book early. When there is not enough gear for everyone, we give priority to schools where more than a third of the pupils get free school meals, and to groups that have never borrowed from us before.

COLLECTING AND RETURNING LOANED GEAR

You collect and return library gear at the Lakeside shop, at the back door next to the workshop, from Monday to Friday between 9:00 and 11:00. Please come with a car or van that is big enough: 15 tents and 40 sleeping bags fill a small van. We can also bring the gear to a school within 30 km of the city for a flat fee of 40 each way.

When you collect, someone from the workshop team goes through a checklist with you. Count the bags together and check that every tent bag holds its poles and pegs. Once you sign the checklist, the gear is the responsibility of your group until it comes back to us.

When you bring the gear back:

Bring it back on the agreed day. If you cannot, call us before 10:00 on that day.

Dry the tents fully before you bring them. If that is not possible after a rainy camp, tell us, and we dry them in the workshop at no cost.

Keep any broken parts and bring them with you. A broken pole is easy to replace; a missing one is not.

Do not wash the sleeping bags yourself. We wash every bag after each loan.

Normal wear and small damage cost you nothing; that is part of lending gear to children. If gear is lost, we ask the school or group to pay 25% of the shop price, up to a maximum of 300 for one loan. If gear comes back damaged on purpose, or very late without a reason, we may say no to the next loan.

Teachers and group leaders can bring their pupils to our free Pitching and packing course before the trip. Tell us when you book the loan, and we keep places for your group.

CLUB DISCOUNTS AND PARTNER CLUBS

Hiking and scouting clubs can sign a partner agreement with us. The members of a partner club then get the club discount that the terms of sale describe. To become a partner club, a club must:

be a registered association with its own bank account;

have at least 25 paying members;

organise at least six walks, camps or other outdoor activities each year.

The club secretary applies on pinewood.example/clubs and uploads the club's statutes and the latest member count. A partner agreement runs for one calendar year. It renews itself every year, unless the club or Pinewood ends it before 1 December.

Each partner club gets its own discount code, and the code changes every year on 1 January. Members type the code in the online store, or show their club membership card at the till and say the code. We only send the code to the secretary, so please share it with your members and not on a public website. If a code turns up on a coupon website, we replace it at once and tell the secretary.

On top of the member discount, a partner club gets:

a free club evening once a year in the talk room of the City centre shop, with a short talk by someone from our test team;

a club order once per season: the club collects the orders of its members, and we send them together to one address, with the club discount and free delivery;

a place on the list of partner clubs on our website, so new walkers can find you.

When the club itself buys gear, for example tents for club camps, it should open a business account. Those orders then follow the business tiers, not the member discount.

STAFF UNIFORMS

Many organisations dress their outdoor staff in our clothing: park rangers, outdoor centres, mountain guides, ferry crews and garden centres. A uniform order is a business order with a few extras.

We keep a fixed uniform range for at least three years, so you can add new staff later in exactly the same colours. The uniform range has the Storm Jacket, the Breeze Jacket, the Ridge Fleece, a softshell vest, a polo shirt of recycled polyester, a cap and a warm hat. Products outside this range can change colour from one season to the next, so we do not advise them for uniforms.

Getting the sizes right:

Step 1: Ask for a size kit. We lend you one piece of each uniform product in every size for two weeks, for free. You only pay the postage to send it back.

Step 2: Let each person try the sizes and write them on our size list. Most organisations do this at a staff meeting.

Step 3: Send us the size list with your order. We pack one parcel for each person, with their name on it, so you can hand them out at once.

For a uniform order for more than 50 people, someone from the business team can visit you with the size kit and help on the day.

You can reorder single pieces at any time in the business portal, in the same colours and with the same logo. For a reorder, the 20-piece minimum for logos does not apply, as long as the logo file and its position stay the same. We keep your logo file and your size list for five years.

When staff leave, you can send their old uniforms back through End of Trail, our take-back programme. We take off the logos first, so your logo never appears on gear that someone else wears.

LOGO PRINTING AND EMBROIDERY

As the terms of sale say, we can put your logo on backpacks and clothing from 20 pieces. Here are the details. We do the work together with a print shop in the same industrial area as our warehouse, so the gear never travels far.

There are two ways to put on a logo:

Embroidery: stitched with thread, very strong, and good for fleeces, caps, hats and polo shirts. It can have up to 8 thread colours, and the logo can be up to 10 by 10 cm.

Transfer print: a thin film pressed on with heat, good for waterproof jackets and backpacks, because stitches would make holes in a waterproof fabric. It can have up to 4 colours.

We never embroider a waterproof jacket. A logo on the Storm Jacket is always a transfer print.

What it costs: a setup fee of 45 for each logo in each position, paid once. After that, embroidery costs from 6 per piece and a transfer print from 4 per piece, depending on the size of the logo. On a reorder with the same logo in the same position, there is no new setup fee.

What we need from you: the logo as a vector file (SVG, EPS, or a PDF with outlined text), the colours as Pantone numbers if you have them, and where you want the logo on each product. A photo or a small JPG is not enough, because it looks blurred when we make it larger.

Before we start, we send you a digital proof that shows the logo on the product in its real size. Nothing is made until you approve the proof in writing, and the time of about three weeks counts from that approval. After approval, the logo cannot be changed. Items with a logo cannot be returned unless they have a fault.

EVENTS AND SPONSORING REQUESTS

Every year we support a small number of events and groups. Our sponsoring budget for 2026 is 18,000. Most of it goes to events that bring people outdoors who do not usually go, and to work that keeps paths and nature in good shape.

What we like to support:

path repair days and litter clean-ups in the hills and along the lakes;

school and youth camps, often together with the School Gear Library;

local walking events, trail runs and orienteering races that are open to beginners;

mountain rescue teams, and first aid training for volunteers.

What we do not support: motor sports, events with hunting or fishing, political parties, and projects for one person, such as a private expedition. We also do not give sponsoring in return for social media posts alone.

How to ask for sponsoring:

Step 1: Fill in the sponsoring form on pinewood.example/sponsoring at least three months before the event. We only look at later requests if there is money left in the budget.

Step 2: Tell us what the event is, how many people take part, what you ask for (money, prizes, gear on loan, or a stand at the event), and what we get in return, if anything.

Step 3: A small group of staff meets on the first Wednesday of every month to look at new requests. You get an answer within four weeks after that meeting.

Prizes from us are worth at most 250 per event. Gear on loan for an event, such as tents for the camp of a long race, is free, but it follows the same rules as a loan from the School Gear Library. If we say no, you are welcome to ask again the next year, but please do not ask twice for the same event in one year.

SUSTAINABILITY REPORT: THE MAIN NUMBERS

We publish a sustainability report every April. It covers the year before, and an outside auditor checks it. You can read the full report on pinewood.example/report, or ask for a printed copy in any shop. The report for 2025 came out on 14 April 2026. These are its main numbers.

Materials:

64% of the fabric in our own clothing was recycled, against 41% in 2021.

All of our down was certified, as it has been every year since 2015.

None of our products had PFAS, the so-called forever chemicals, in their water-repellent finish. We stopped using PFAS in 2023.

Climate:

Our total emissions were 1,920 tonnes of CO2 equivalent. That is 23% lower than in 2021, the year we use as our starting point.

71% of these emissions came from making our products, 17% from transport, and 12% from our shops, our office and our warehouse.

The solar panels on the warehouse roof made 310,000 kWh of electricity, about 58% of what the warehouse used.

Repair and reuse:

Our workshop did 5,860 repairs. Of these, 2,140 were on products of other brands.

Through Second Trail we sold 3,470 used items for a second life.

End of Trail, our take-back programme, collected 6.3 tonnes of worn-out gear.

The School Gear Library made 212 loans to 148 different groups.

The report also says what went wrong. In 2025 our air freight went up, because a delivery of tents left the factory late, and we flew 14 tonnes of goods to have them in the shops before the summer. We count these emissions in full, and we do not hide them with offsets.

SUSTAINABILITY GOALS AND HOW WE MEASURE

In 2022 we set four goals for the year 2030:

at least 85% recycled or renewable fabric in our own clothing;

emissions at half of what they were in 2021;

one repair in our workshop for every ten products we sell;

no air freight at all, except for spare parts that a customer is waiting for.

We measure our emissions with the Greenhouse Gas Protocol. We include the emissions of the factories that make our products, and of every parcel we send, not only our own buildings. We do not buy carbon offsets to reach our goals. What we cannot cut yet, we report as it is.

Our products are made in nine factories in Portugal, Romania, Lithuania and Vietnam. Someone from our team visits each factory at least once a year, and the report lists every factory with its address. A factory that refuses an outside check of its working conditions cannot work for us. Since 2024, every factory pays at least a living wage, as checked by the same outside auditor.

When we find a mistake in an earlier report, we correct it in the next one and say so openly. For example, in the 2025 report we raised the transport emissions for 2023 by 40 tonnes, after a carrier sent us better data.

How you can help: use your gear for longer, repair before you replace, and choose standard delivery or collection in a shop when you are not in a hurry. One parcel with several items is better than several parcels, so if you can, wait and order everything at once.

PACKAGING

We send online orders in cardboard boxes made of at least 90% recycled fibre, closed with paper tape. We fill empty space with crumpled paper, never with plastic air cushions or foam. Since March 2023, clothing comes in a paper bag instead of a plastic one, and labels hang on cotton thread instead of plastic tags.

The warehouse system picks one of six box sizes for each order, so a pair of gloves does not travel in a box made for a tent. Small orders up to 2 kg go in a padded paper envelope instead of a box.

Some products still need plastic. Down sleeping bags travel in a thin bag of recycled plastic, because the down must stay dry. Tents come in their own stuff sack, which you keep and use. Gas cartridges are not sent by post at all.

Using our boxes again: every box has a second strip of tape inside the lid, so you can close it again if you send something back. If you collect your order in a shop, you can ask the staff to unpack it there. We take the box back and use it for the next order that goes to a shop.

Business orders: for large orders we can send the gear on a pallet with a pallet cover that can be used again, instead of plastic film. We pick up the cover when we deliver your next order. On request we also send business orders with no bag around each item, which works well for uniforms that you hand out at once.

If our packaging was much too big for what was inside, please tell us through the contact form and add a photo. The warehouse team reads every message about packaging. In 2025, these messages led us to add our smallest box size.

TAKE-BACK PROGRAMME FOR WORN-OUT GEAR

Second Trail is for gear that can still be used. For gear that is really worn out, we have End of Trail, our take-back programme, which started in 2024. You can give us outdoor clothing, tents, sleeping bags, backpacks and walking shoes, of any brand and in any condition, and we make sure they do not end up in the rubbish.

How it works:

Step 1: Wash clothing and sleeping bags, or at least shake out sand and dirt. Wet or mouldy items cannot be sorted, and they spoil the rest of the bag.

Step 2: Put the items in a bag and drop it in the green End of Trail box in any of our shops in Europe. You do not need a receipt or an account.

Step 3: Show your Trail Club card at the till. You get a voucher of 5 for each full bag, for up to four bags a year.

What happens next: the workshop sorts the boxes once a week. Items that can still be repaired go to Second Trail or to the School Gear Library. Clothing that is still wearable but that we cannot sell goes to a night shelter for homeless people in the city. Zips, buckles and poles are kept as spare parts for repairs. Everything else goes to a recycling partner, where the fabric is shredded and made into insulation for buildings.

What we cannot take: underwear and socks, gas cartridges and stoves, batteries and headlamps (please bring these to a battery collection point), climbing ropes, and anything that is wet.

The Riyadh store does not have an End of Trail box yet. We plan to add one in 2027, once we have found a recycling partner in Saudi Arabia.

CAREERS AND HOW TO APPLY

We are a small company, so we only have a few open jobs each year: in the shops, in the workshop, in the warehouse, in the test team and in the office. Every open job is listed on pinewood.example/jobs. We do not use recruitment agencies, and we do not answer agencies that write to us.

How to apply:

Step 1: Choose a job on the jobs page and click Apply. Send your CV and a short letter of at most one page about why you want the job. A letter about one good trip tells us more than a long list of skills.

Step 2: Within ten working days you hear from us, also when the answer is no.

Step 3: If your letter fits the job, we invite you to a video call of about 20 minutes.

Step 4: Next comes a paid trial half-day in the team you want to join. In a shop, you help real customers; in the workshop, you repair a real product.

Step 5: You get our decision within one week after the trial half-day.

We keep your application for four weeks after the job is filled, and then delete it, unless you agree that we keep it for one year for other jobs.

Open applications: if no open job fits you, you can still send an open application to jobs@pinewood.example. We read every one, but we only answer when a fitting job comes up.

For jobs in the Riyadh store, apply on the same page. The interviews take place in Riyadh, and every job in that store needs Arabic. For the other jobs, good English is enough to start, and we help you learn the rest.

WORKING AT PINEWOOD

Every member of staff gets:

40% off our gear for themselves, and 20% off for close family, up to 2,000 in shop prices a year;

two paid test days a year to try gear outdoors, with a short report afterwards for the test team;

one free place a year on a guided trip, on a date that still has a free place;

a clothing allowance of 300 a year for staff in the shops and the workshop, for the clothes they wear at work.

Working hours: a full-time job is 36 hours a week. Shop staff work on a four-week rota, with at most one Sunday shift in every four weeks. Staff in the Airport shop can choose between early and late shifts, because that shop opens at 7:00 and closes at 22:00.

Training: every new member of staff spends the first week in three places: a shop, the workshop and the warehouse, so they see how the whole company works. Shop staff take a two-day product course twice a year, before the summer season and before the winter season. We pay for first aid training for everyone. Staff who want to become guides on our trips get half of the cost of the mountain leader course paid.

Learning by doing: everyone in the office, including the managing director, works at least one day a month in a shop during the busy months, which are May to August and December. It is the fastest way to hear what customers really ask.

Staff are also invited to the yearly staff trip in September: two days outdoors with everyone from every team, while temporary staff keep the shops open.

SHOP ACCESSIBILITY: ENTRANCES AND INSIDE THE SHOPS

We want every shop to be easy to visit, also for people who use a wheelchair, walk with a stick, push a pram or have low vision. This is how our shops are set up in September 2026.

Lakeside shop: the main entrance on Harbour Road has a gentle ramp and an automatic door, so you do not need to take any steps. The whole shop is on one floor. The rental desk and the Second Trail corner are at the back, with wide aisles on the way.

City centre shop: the entrance on Market Street is level with the pavement. The shop has two floors. The ground floor has clothing and shoes, and the first floor has tents, sleeping bags and the talk room. A lift that is big enough for a wheelchair and a helper goes between the two floors. If the lift is out of order, we say so on the shop page of our website, and staff bring products down to you.

Airport shop: the shop is in the arrivals hall of Terminal 2, on the same level as the exit from the baggage hall. There are no steps and no doors.

In every shop:

one fitting room is larger, with a seat and a handrail;

there are chairs near the tills and in the shoe area;

every till has a hearing loop (look for the ear sign);

staff are happy to read labels and prices out loud, and to reach products on high shelves.

If you need help, ask any member of staff, or call the shop before your visit and tell us when you plan to come. We then make sure that someone has time for you. Our website meets the WCAG 2.2 standard at level AA. If a page is hard to use, please tell us through the contact form.

In December, when the queues are long, the City centre and Lakeside shops have a separate wrapping table from 1 to 24 December, staffed by our team.

Online, you can add gift wrapping to any item in your basket for 3.50 per item. The item arrives wrapped inside the normal box, with a card that holds your own message of up to 200 characters. When you choose gift wrapping, no prices are shown on the delivery note in the box; we send the invoice to your email address instead.

What we do not wrap: items longer than 70 cm, and backpacks of 60 litres or more. For these, we add a wide cotton ribbon and a gift tag instead, at no cost. Gift cards come in a free paper envelope with space for a message, so they need no wrapping.

Gift receipts: in the shop and online, you can ask for a gift receipt. It shows the item but not the price. The person who gets the present can bring the item back to any shop with the gift receipt. They get the value on a gift card, and we do not tell you about it.

Christmas returns: for presents bought between 15 November and 24 December, the return period runs until 31 January of the next year, also when 30 days have already passed. The item must still be unused and have all its labels, as with every return.

If you want a surprise to stay a surprise, choose a delivery address that is not the address of the person who gets the gift, or collect the parcel in a shop.

PERSONAL SHOPPING APPOINTMENTS

If you need a lot of new gear, or you are not sure what to choose, you can book a personal shopping appointment. A member of staff who knows the gear well takes time just for you. Appointments are free, and you do not have to buy anything.

There are three kinds of appointment:

Gear advice, 45 minutes: for one question, such as a first tent, a sleeping bag for winter, or a pair of walking boots.

Full kit, 90 minutes: for someone who is starting from nothing, or who is getting ready for a big trip like the High route. We go through your packing list together, from your boots to your headlamp.

Backpack fitting, 30 minutes: we measure your back, fill a backpack with 12 kg of sandbags, and let you walk around the shop until it feels right.

When and where: appointments are in the Lakeside and City centre shops, on weekdays, while the shop is quiet. The first appointment of the day starts when the shop opens, and the last one starts at 15:00. The Airport shop does not offer appointments.

How to book: use pinewood.example/appointments at least two days ahead. Choose the shop, the kind of appointment and a time, and tell us in a few words what you are looking for. You can also book by phone through the support team. If you cannot come, please cancel at least 24 hours before, so someone else can take your time.

Bring the gear you already have, such as your old sleeping bag or the boots you plan to wear. It helps us see what works for you and what does not. Business customers can book a group appointment for up to six staff, for example to choose a uniform together.

LOST PROPERTY IN OUR SHOPS

If you lost something in one of our shops, there is a good chance that we have it. At closing time every day, staff check the fitting rooms, the talk room and the shop floor. Every item they find goes into the lost property box of that shop, with a note of the date and the place where it was found.

How to get it back:

Step 1: Call the shop or ask at the till as soon as you can. If you cannot come, use the contact form and choose Lost property. Tell us which shop, which day, and what the item looks like.

Step 2: The shop staff look in the box and tell you within two working days whether they found it.

Step 3: Collect the item in the shop. Bring an identity card if the item is valuable, such as a phone, a wallet or keys.

We can also post an item to you if you pay the postage in advance. We do not post phones, keys or bank cards; you must collect those yourself.

How long we keep things:

Phones, wallets, bank cards, identity cards and keys: 7 days, then we hand them to the local police. In the Airport shop, these items go to the airport's own lost and found office on the same day.

Clothing, bottles, umbrellas and everything else: 30 days. After that, clean clothing goes to the same night shelter that gets clothing from End of Trail, and we recycle or throw away the rest.

Rental gear that a customer leaves behind in a shop is not lost property. It goes straight back to the rental desk, and the normal rental rules apply.

COMPLAINTS PROCEDURE AND ESCALATION

We want to hear about it when something went wrong. A complaint helps us fix the cause, not only your case. This is how a complaint moves through our company, step by step.

Step 1, the first answer: send your complaint through the contact form with the topic Complaint, or hand it in on paper in any shop. Add your order number, or the shop and the date of your visit, and say what you would like us to do. You get a reference number by email that starts with C, for example C-26-0412. As our terms of sale say, a team lead then answers within five working days.

Step 2, a second look: if you do not agree with the answer of the team lead, reply to the same email within 30 days and ask for a review. Our customer service manager, Daniel Okafor, then looks at the whole case again, talks to the staff who were involved, and answers within ten working days. Most complaints end here.

Step 3, the managing director: if you are still not happy, you can ask for your case to go to the managing director. She reads the whole file and answers in writing within 15 working days. Her answer is the final answer of Pinewood Outdoor.

Step 4, outside help: after step 3, or if we did not keep to these times, you can take the complaint to an outside body, as the terms of sale explain. Business customers can ask for mediation instead.

Complaints about a member of staff go straight to the customer service manager, from step 1. We keep complaint files for two years. Every three months the management reads a report about them: what went wrong, how often, and what we changed. Nobody is ever refused service because they complained.

DATA REQUESTS

You have the right to know what personal data we hold about you, and to have it corrected, moved or deleted. You can do most of this yourself in your account, as the help centre explains. For everything else, send us a data request.

The data requests we handle:

Access: a copy of all data we hold about you, including shop purchases linked to your Trail Club card, chat conversations and complaint files.

Correction: when something is wrong, such as a misspelt name or an old address.

Deletion: when you want us to delete data that the law does not ask us to keep.

Portability: your order history as a CSV file that you can take somewhere else.

Objection: when you do not want us to use your data for a certain purpose, such as personal offers in the newsletter.

How to send a request: write to our data protection officer, Mirjam de Wit, at privacy@pinewood.example, or by post to 20 Mill Lane with the words Data request on the envelope. To protect your data, we only reply to the email address of your account. If you write from another address, we first ask you to confirm the request from your account address. We never ask for a copy of your passport.

We answer within one month. If a request is very large, we may need two more months, but we tell you so within the first month. Data requests are always free.

Camera images: our shops have cameras at the entrances and the tills. We keep the images for 28 days, and then they are deleted automatically, unless the police need them for a case. If you want the images of yourself, tell us the shop, the date and the time within those 28 days.

For business accounts, the data of the organisation, such as its invoices, belongs to the organisation. A buyer who leaves can ask us to remove their name and login, but not the orders they placed.

OUR HISTORY: 2009 TO 2016

Pinewood Outdoor was founded in the spring of 2009 by Anna Lindqvist and Pieter Brandsma. Anna had worked as a mountain guide for eight years. Pieter was an engineer who fixed his friends' tents in his garage at the weekend. Their first business was the small rental shop by the lake, in an old boat shed, with 20 tents, 30 sleeping bags and a sewing machine they borrowed from Pieter's mother. The name comes from the pine wood behind the shed.

In the first summer, most customers were walkers who came by train and did not want to carry a tent all the way from home. Many tents came back with broken poles and torn zips, and Pieter fixed them at night. That is how our workshop began: from the very first year, repair was part of the business.

2011: the workshop moved into its own building next to the boat shed, and started to repair gear of other brands too.

2012: our first own product, a 60-litre backpack, made in a small factory in Portugal. The first 200 sold out in five weeks.

2013: the online store opened, with 40 products and delivery in one country only.

2014: the Trail 2 tent came out after two years of testing on real trips.

2015: the boat shed made way for the Lakeside shop as it stands today, with the workshop next door. From this year on, all our down has been certified.

2016: the City centre shop opened on Market Street, and our very first guided trip, a weekend by the lake, took 6 people.

OUR HISTORY: 2017 TO TODAY

2017: a teacher asked if she could borrow tents for a class camp. Her question was the start of the School Gear Library.

2018: the Airport shop opened in Terminal 2, for travellers who forget or lose gear on the way. Its long opening hours follow the times of the first and last flights.

2019: the office and the warehouse moved to 20 Mill Lane, and we started to deliver to the whole European Union.

2020: in the spring, all our shops were closed for nine weeks during the pandemic. The workshop kept going and repaired 1,100 products that customers sent in by post. Second Trail started in the autumn of that year.

2021: our first sustainability report. We still use 2021 as the starting point for our climate goals.

2022: Trail Club replaced the old paper stamp card. In its first year, 18,000 people joined.

2023: we stopped using PFAS and plastic clothing bags, and the solar panels went onto the warehouse roof.

2024: our first store outside Europe opened in Riyadh, with a local team that knows desert camping, its own workshop and its own rental desk. In the same year, the green End of Trail boxes arrived in our shops in Europe.

2025: the workshop did more repairs than in any year before.

2026: business customers got their own business portal, with personal logins for every buyer.

Anna Lindqvist is still our managing director. Pieter Brandsma stopped his daily work in 2021, but he still leads the test team on one long trip every year, and he still helps out in the workshop when it is busy.

Some things have not changed since 2009. We sell gear that we have tested ourselves, we fix what breaks, and we believe the best piece of gear is the one you already own.
