# Pinewood Outdoor: self-guided hiking routes near our shops

Not everyone wants to walk with a guide. For people who like to go on their own, our test team has walked and written up eighteen routes in the hills, woods and lakes around our shops. Most of them start at a bus stop or a train station, so you can leave the car at home. Every route below uses the same layout: where it starts and how to get there, how long it is, how much you climb, how long it takes, its grade, where you find water, where you may camp, what to watch out for, and which Pinewood shop is the closest if you need gear on the way.

The times in this guide are walking times for an adult with a day pack, without long breaks. Add about one third for a heavy backpack, for children, or for a group. The heights are the total climb over the whole route, not the height of the highest point.

We check each route once a year, in spring. Paths change, bridges wash away and bus timetables move, so always look at the latest bus or train times before you leave, and read the weather forecast for the hills, not only for the city. The routes are free to use. If you find a mistake in this guide, tell us, and we fix it in the next version.

## How the route grades work

Every route in this guide has one of four grades. The grade tells you how hard the ground is, not how long the day is, so a long Green route can still be a tiring day. Our grades are not the same as the levels of our guided trips.

- **Green:** wide paths and forest roads with no steep or rough parts. Good for families, prams on some routes, and people who have not walked in the hills before. Trainers are fine in dry weather.
- **Blue:** narrow paths with some steep parts, roots and stones. You need walking shoes with a good grip, and you should be able to walk for four or five hours.
- **Red:** steep and rough paths, some short places where you use your hands, and long stretches far from a road. You need walking boots, a map, and some experience in the hills. Do not walk a Red route alone the first time.
- **Black:** high mountain ground, with scrambling, snow that can stay into summer, and no easy way down in bad weather. Only for people who can read a map well, who have walked several Red routes, and who know when to turn back.

The paths are marked with painted stripes in the colour of their grade, on trees, rocks and posts. Where two routes share a path, you see both colours. On Black routes, the marks can be far apart and hard to see in fog or snow, so do not walk them by the paint alone.

## Wild camping in the region

Many people ask where they may put up a tent. The rules in our region are stricter than in some other countries, so please read this part before you plan a night out.

Wild camping means one night in a small tent, away from a camp site. In our region it is allowed only in the places that each route below names, and on the open high ground above 1,600 metres. It is not allowed in the nature reserves, in the city forest, on farmland, or within 500 metres of a road or a house. A fire is never allowed when you wild camp; use a gas stove and keep it on stones or bare ground, never on dry grass.

Arrive late and leave early. Put up your tent after 19:00 and take it down before 9:00. You may stay one night in the same place; on the second night you must move at least two kilometres. Groups of more than six tents must use a camp site.

The park rangers check the most popular spots in summer, mainly around the lakes and on the plateau. A tent in the wrong place costs a fine of 150, and the rangers can ask you to pack up at any time of night. If you are not sure whether a spot is allowed, choose a camp site; the routes below name one where there is one close.

Bivvy bags and hammocks count as camping too. The rules are the same as for a tent.

## What to do in a thunderstorm

Summer storms in our hills come fast, often in the afternoon after a hot morning. The ridges and the plateau are the most dangerous places, because you are the highest point around. Plan long routes so you are off the high ground by about 14:00 when storms are in the forecast.

Count the seconds between the flash and the thunder. If the time is less than thirty seconds, the storm is close enough to reach you, and you should act now, not when it starts to rain.

- Get off ridges, summits and open high ground at once. Go down, even if it means leaving the route.
- Stay away from single trees, metal fences, wire ropes and chains. On the Haldane Gorge and the Corran Ridge, let go of the chains and wait away from them.
- Do not shelter in a shallow cave or under a rock overhang. Power from a strike can jump across the opening.
- If you are in a group, spread out so that people are at least fifteen metres apart. Then one strike cannot hurt everyone.
- If you cannot get lower, crouch on your backpack or sleeping mat with your feet together, your head down and your hands over your ears. Do not lie flat on the ground.
- Walking poles are not a danger in your hand, but lay them down if your hair stands up or you hear a buzzing sound.

Wait thirty minutes after the last thunder before you go back up.

## Leave no trace

The hills around our shops are busy, and every walker leaves a mark. These seven rules keep the paths and the camp spots nice for the next person. We teach them on every course, and our test team follows them on every route in this guide.

1. **Plan ahead.** Know the route, the weather and the rules before you go, so you do not have to camp or cut across in a place where it is not allowed.
2. **Stay on the path.** Short cuts on bends wear away the ground, and the next rain turns them into gullies. Walk through a muddy patch, not around it, so the path does not get wider.
3. **Take everything home.** This includes fruit peel, tea bags and tissues. Peel takes years to break down in the cold and dry air of the plateau.
4. **Go to the toilet far from water.** Stay at least 50 metres away from lakes and streams. Dig a hole 15 to 20 cm deep, and take your toilet paper back out in a bag.
5. **Leave what you find.** Do not pick flowers, move stones or build cairns. Cairns that walkers build lead other people off the real path.
6. **Keep fires out of the hills.** Cook on a gas stove. Old fire rings are not an invitation; do not use them.
7. **Respect animals and other people.** Keep dogs on a lead near sheep and cattle, close every gate behind you, and keep music to your headphones.

## Downloading the free GPX files

Every route in this guide has a free GPX file, the track you can load into a phone app or a GPS device. The file has the full line of the route, the water points, the allowed camp spots and the bus or train stop at the start.

To download a file, open **Trips & courses** on our website and choose **Self-guided routes**. Click the route, then **Download GPX**. You do not need an account, and the files are free for personal use. Please do not sell them or put them on other websites; link to our page instead, so people always get the latest version.

Most phone apps open a GPX file when you tap it. If yours does not, open the app first and choose **Import**. On a GPS device, copy the file into the folder named **GPX** when the device is connected to a computer by cable.

When we change a route, for example because a path is closed, we upload a new file and change the date in the file name. Download the file again before every trip if your copy is older than the date on the website.

A phone is not enough on its own on Red and Black routes. Cold weather drains the battery fast, and there is no signal in the Haldane Gorge or on most of the Wenmoor plateau. Take the paper map as well, and keep the phone warm in an inside pocket. If you do not have a map, the shop staff can print the route for you for free.

## Reporting a blocked or damaged path

If a tree has fallen across a path, a bridge is broken, a sign is missing or a path is closed and the guide does not say so, please tell us. We pass every report on to the park rangers within one working day, and we warn other walkers on the route page.

You can report a problem in three ways:

- Open the route page on our website and click **Report a problem**. You can add photos and drop a pin on the map.
- Write to us in the chat, and give the route name and roughly where the problem is.
- Tell the staff in any of our shops. They fill in the report with you.

The more exact you are, the faster the rangers can fix it. A photo, the time you saw it, and a place are the most useful. The best place is the GPS position from your phone app; the second best is the nearest sign post or the distance from the start. Please do not try to clear big trees or move rocks yourself.

If the problem is dangerous right now, for example a landslide that has buried part of the path, call the mountain rescue on 112 first and report it to us afterwards. Do not walk past a closed sign, even if the path looks fine.

As a thank-you, every walker who sends a useful report gets 50 Trail Club points once the rangers confirm it.

## Harbour Loop

A gentle walk that starts and ends at our Lakeside shop. It follows the shore of the lake past the old boat sheds, climbs gently through a pine wood to a bench with a view over the whole lake, and comes back along the harbour wall. It is the route our shop staff send people to when they want to try new walking boots before a longer trip.

- **Start:** the door of the Lakeside shop. You need no bus; the shop has a small car park, free for customers for up to four hours.
- **Distance:** 6.5 km, a loop.
- **Height gained:** 180 m.
- **Time:** about 2 hours.
- **Grade:** Green.
- **Water:** there is a drinking tap outside the rowing club, at the halfway point of the loop. It runs all year.
- **Camping:** not allowed anywhere on this loop, because the whole shore near the town is a park.
- **Watch out:** the wooden boardwalk along the reeds is very slippery on frosty mornings. In spring, when the snow melts in the hills, the lake can flood the lowest part of the shore path; then follow the yellow signs up to the road for 400 metres.
- **Nearest shop:** Lakeside, at the start and the end.

Bring a coin for the coffee machine at the rowing club, and walk the loop the other way round in the late afternoon for the sunset over the water.

## Grey Tarn Circuit

A popular half-day walk to a small, dark lake in a bowl below the cliffs of Hollin Fell. The path climbs through farmland and then a steep, stony gully, goes all the way round the tarn, and comes back down by a gentler path on the other side of the valley. The view back over the valley from the lip of the bowl is one of the best in the region.

- **Start:** the bus stop at Hollin village green. Take bus 41 from the city centre bus station; the ride takes about 50 minutes, and buses go every hour.
- **Distance:** 11.8 km, a loop.
- **Height gained:** 520 m.
- **Time:** about 4 and a half hours.
- **Grade:** Blue.
- **Water:** the only water on the way is the stream that runs out of Grey Tarn. Filter or boil it before you drink, because sheep graze around the tarn.
- **Camping:** one night is allowed on the flat grass at the east shore of the tarn. It is a beautiful spot, and it fills up fast on summer weekends.
- **Watch out:** cows with young calves graze in the lower fields from May to September. Keep your dog on a short lead, walk calmly round the herd, and let go of the lead if the cows come at you.
- **Nearest shop:** City centre, a five-minute walk from the bus station.

The gully is the only steep part. Go up it, not down, so the loose stones are less of a problem.

## Three Lakes Ridge

The day version of the ridge path that our guided Lakes and ridges trip walks over three days. You follow the long grassy ridge above the three lakes from one end to the other, with water on both sides below you nearly all day. It is a big day out, and you need to be fit, but the path is clear and easy to follow in good weather.

- **Start:** Elsby Halt, a small train station. Take the local train towards the lakes from the central station; it takes 35 minutes. The route ends at Birchmere, where the lake ferry or bus 23 takes you back to the Lakeside shop.
- **Distance:** 16.4 km, from one end to the other.
- **Height gained:** 910 m.
- **Time:** about 6 and a half hours.
- **Grade:** Red.
- **Water:** there is none on the ridge itself. Fill every bottle at the fountain in front of Elsby church before you start; we suggest at least two litres per person on a warm day.
- **Camping:** wild camping is not allowed on the ridge. Stay at the Birchmere camp site at the end, which costs 8 per tent a night and has hot showers.
- **Watch out:** the ridge has no shelter at all. In a strong wind from the west, leave the crest at the second saddle and take the lower path along the shore.
- **Nearest shop:** Lakeside, about 25 minutes by bus from the end of the route.

## Kessel Pass

Our hardest route, and the only one that crosses the main mountain chain. It climbs from the valley at Fennhausen through larch woods and high meadows to Kessel Pass at 2,140 metres, then drops on the far side to the Kessel hut. Most people walk up to the pass and come back the same way, but strong walkers can sleep at the hut and come back the next day.

- **Start:** the train station at Fennhausen. The direct train from the central station takes about two hours, so take the first train of the day.
- **Distance:** 19.6 km there and back.
- **Height gained:** 1,240 m.
- **Time:** about 8 hours.
- **Grade:** Black.
- **Water:** a spring next to the Kessel hut runs from June to September. Higher up there is only snowmelt, which you must treat.
- **Camping:** wild camping is allowed on the open ground above 1,600 metres, on both sides of the pass. The hut also has a room for walkers, which you must book ahead.
- **Watch out:** on the north side of the pass, old snow often lies across the path until the end of June. A slip there is dangerous. Carry an ice axe and know how to use it, or wait for July.
- **Nearest shop:** City centre, five minutes on foot from the central station where the train leaves, so it is the best place to pick up last-minute gear.

The last train back from Fennhausen leaves at 19:40. If you miss it, there is a small inn next to the station.

## Fennhausen Meadows

An easy walk for the day you arrive in Fennhausen, or for a rest day between harder routes. It goes out of the village through hay meadows full of flowers, along a clear mountain stream, and back past the old wooden church. Families with small children like it, and there are benches every kilometre or so.

- **Start:** the train station at Fennhausen. The walk starts on the far side of the tracks; cross by the footbridge at the end of the platform.
- **Distance:** 7.9 km, a loop.
- **Height gained:** 240 m.
- **Time:** about 2 and a half hours.
- **Grade:** Green.
- **Water:** the old stone pump next to the church gives clean drinking water. The stream is clear, but cattle graze higher up, so do not drink from it.
- **Camping:** not allowed in the meadows. The Fennhausen camp site is next to the church, open from May to October, and takes tents without booking.
- **Watch out:** the meadows are cut for hay only once a year, in late July. Until then, the farmers ask you to walk in single file and stay on the path, because tall grass that people have walked on cannot be cut. Dogs are not welcome in the meadows in June and July.
- **Nearest shop:** City centre, two hours away by train. There is no outdoor shop in Fennhausen, so bring everything you need.

In early summer, look for orchids along the stream. Please do not pick them.

## Marten Falls

A short and steep walk up a wooded valley to the highest waterfall in the region, where the stream drops 40 metres into a round pool. Wooden steps and two small bridges lead you up the side of the falls to a viewing platform at the top, and a quiet forest road brings you back down.

- **Start:** the bus stop at Larkhope, a small village beyond the airport. Bus 7 goes from the stop in front of the Airport shop every half hour; the ride takes about 25 minutes.
- **Distance:** 9.3 km, a loop.
- **Height gained:** 410 m.
- **Time:** about 3 and a half hours.
- **Grade:** Blue.
- **Water:** carry all you need. The stream looks clean, but it runs past a dairy farm above the falls, and we do not advise drinking it, even filtered.
- **Camping:** not allowed. The whole valley is a nature reserve, and the rangers check it often.
- **Watch out:** the spray from the falls keeps the steps wet all year, and they get very slippery with moss and, in winter, with ice. After heavy rain, the lower bridge is closed when the water rises over the warning mark; then go up and down by the forest road.
- **Nearest shop:** Airport, at the other end of the bus 7 line.

The pool at the bottom looks good for a swim, but it is very cold and deep, with strong currents under the falls. Swimming there is not allowed.

## Corran Ridge Traverse

A long and serious mountain day along the sharp rocky ridge that joins Corran Fell to Blackcomb. The middle part, called the Corran Teeth, is a row of rock towers where you climb up and down with your hands for about an hour. Fixed chains help in the two hardest places. The views are wide, but there is no easy way off once you are on the Teeth.

- **Start:** the bus stop at Rothwick. Take bus 41 from the city centre bus station, past Hollin, to the end of the line; the ride takes 1 hour and 10 minutes.
- **Distance:** 21.3 km, a loop back to Rothwick.
- **Height gained:** 1,380 m.
- **Time:** about 9 hours.
- **Grade:** Black, for the scramble over the Teeth.
- **Water:** there is no water for 14 km, from the last farm below Corran Fell to the tarn at Blackcomb. Carry at least three litres.
- **Camping:** wild camping is allowed on the wide grassy saddle before the Teeth, which lets you split the route over two days.
- **Watch out:** between the first and the last tower there is no way down to the valley. Do not start the Teeth after 12:00, and do not start the route at all when thunderstorms are in the forecast.
- **Nearest shop:** City centre, where bus 41 starts.

The rock is good and rough when dry, but it gets slippery with rain. Gloves help with the chains.

## Ashdown Woods

A flat walk through the old beech and oak trees of the city forest, on wide gravel paths. It is the closest route to the city, and you can easily walk it after work in summer. The path passes a small pond and the forest lodge, a café with a terrace.

- **Start:** the Ashdown stop, at the end of tram line 3. The tram leaves from Market Street, right in front of our City centre shop, and the ride takes 20 minutes.
- **Distance:** 5.4 km, a loop.
- **Height gained:** 120 m.
- **Time:** about 1 and a half hours.
- **Grade:** Green.
- **Water:** the forest lodge café has a free water tap for walkers. The café is open from Wednesday to Sunday, from 10:00 to 18:00.
- **Camping:** not allowed. The city forest is closed to tents and to fires, and the park keepers patrol in the evenings.
- **Watch out:** from November to February the forest workers fell trees in parts of the woods. Signs show when a path is closed; follow the diversion even if you see nobody working, because the tree can fall later in the day.
- **Nearest shop:** City centre, at the other end of the tram line.

The paths are wide and even enough for a pram or a wheelchair with large wheels, except for one set of steps near the pond, which you can avoid by the side path.

## Birchmere Shore

A lake walk with a boat ride at the start. You take the ferry from the pier in front of the Lakeside shop across the water to Birchmere, then walk back along the quiet south shore, through small villages and orchards. There is a beach halfway, where you can swim in summer.

- **Start:** Birchmere jetty. The lake ferry leaves the pier near the Lakeside shop every hour from May to October; the crossing takes 40 minutes and costs 6 for an adult, one way.
- **Distance:** 12.6 km, one way back to the Lakeside shop.
- **Height gained:** 290 m.
- **Time:** about 4 hours.
- **Grade:** Green.
- **Water:** there is a drinking fountain on Birchmere jetty, and the beach kiosk at the halfway point sells drinks in summer.
- **Camping:** not allowed along the shore. The Birchmere camp site, five minutes from the jetty, takes tents from April to October.
- **Watch out:** the last ferry back leaves Birchmere at 17:30. If you walk the route the other way round and miss it, you have to walk another 9 km on the road or wait for bus 23, which runs only every two hours in the evening.
- **Nearest shop:** Lakeside, at the end of the walk.

From November to April the ferry does not run. You can still walk the route, but you start with bus 23 to Birchmere instead of the boat.

## Haldane Gorge

A narrow gorge where a river has cut deep into the limestone. The path runs on wooden walkways fixed to the rock, high above the water, and climbs out at the top end on steel ladders and a short section with chains. It is not long, but it is not for people who are afraid of heights.

- **Start:** Haldane Bridge. Take bus 12 from the Airport bus station; the ride takes about 35 minutes. Get off at the stone bridge, before the village.
- **Distance:** 8.7 km, a loop.
- **Height gained:** 460 m.
- **Time:** about 3 and a half hours.
- **Grade:** Red, because of the ladders and the drops.
- **Water:** there is none in the gorge you can reach safely. Bring enough from the start.
- **Camping:** not allowed in the gorge or on the plateau above it.
- **Watch out:** the gorge is closed from 1 November to 30 April, because frost breaks rocks loose from the walls. Even in summer, wear a helmet; the rangers lend them for free at the hut at the entrance.
- **Nearest shop:** Airport, at the bus station where bus 12 starts.

Children under 10 are not allowed on the ladders. Phones have no signal in the gorge, so tell someone your plan before you go in, and do not go in when heavy rain is forecast; the river can rise fast.

## Wenmoor Plateau Crossing

A crossing of the wide, empty plateau to the east of Fennhausen, the same high ground our guided High route walks over. There are no villages and few signs, only heather, peat, small lakes and wide views. Most people do it in two days, with a wild camp in the middle, but fit walkers can cross in one long day.

- **Start:** Wenmoor car park. From the train station at Fennhausen, take bus 90 for 30 minutes to the end of the line. The route ends at the Aldmoor bus stop, with buses back to Fennhausen.
- **Distance:** 23.5 km, from one end to the other.
- **Height gained:** 780 m.
- **Time:** about 9 hours, or two easy days.
- **Grade:** Red, because you need to find your own way.
- **Water:** there are many tarns and streams. The water is brown from the peat but safe once you boil it or use a filter.
- **Camping:** wild camping is allowed anywhere on the plateau, as long as you are more than 500 metres from the road.
- **Watch out:** fog can come down in minutes, and in fog every part of the plateau looks the same. You need a map and a compass, and you need to know how to use them. The ground is wet, so walk in boots with gaiters.
- **Nearest shop:** City centre, near the station where trains to Fennhausen leave.

## Stavely Fort Walk

A walk to the ruins of an old stone fort on a hill above the airport, with a view over the runways and the whole valley. Plane spotters love it. The path goes up through fields, round the walls of the fort, and back down through a beech wood.

- **Start:** the bus stop at Stavely village. Bus 7 from the Airport shop gets there in about 15 minutes, before it goes on to Larkhope.
- **Distance:** 10.2 km, a loop.
- **Height gained:** 330 m.
- **Time:** about 3 hours.
- **Grade:** Blue.
- **Water:** a fountain at the main gate of the fort gives drinking water from April to October. In winter it is turned off.
- **Camping:** not allowed. The fort is a protected monument, and the field around it belongs to a farm.
- **Watch out:** the army uses the land to the north of the fort as a shooting range. On days with shooting, red flags fly on the posts along the path, and the north part of the loop is closed. Shooting is usually on Tuesdays and Thursdays. Then walk to the fort and back the same way.
- **Nearest shop:** Airport, about 15 minutes by bus.

Inside the walls there is a small museum, open on weekends in summer. Entry is free, and it has toilets.

## Larkhope Hill

A short, steep climb to the top of a round, grassy hill with the best sunrise view in the region. Many people walk it in the dark and wait at the top for the sun to come up over the lakes. Take a headlamp and a warm layer for the top, where it is cold and windy even in summer.

- **Start:** the bus stop at Larkhope, the same stop as the Marten Falls route. Bus 7 from the Airport shop takes about 25 minutes; the first bus leaves at 5:10.
- **Distance:** 4.8 km, up and back the same way.
- **Height gained:** 350 m.
- **Time:** about 2 hours.
- **Grade:** Blue.
- **Water:** none at all. Fill up before you leave.
- **Camping:** a tent is not allowed, but you may sleep on the top in a bivvy bag without a tent, for one night, if you leave before 8:00. Many people do this in summer to wake up for the sunrise.
- **Watch out:** the upper part of the path is loose gravel on a steep slope. Going down is harder than going up, mainly in the dark. Walking poles help a lot.
- **Nearest shop:** Airport. It is open every day until late, so you can buy a headlamp or batteries the evening before.

On clear days you can see the snow on Kessel Pass from the top.

## Old Mine Trail

A route through the history of the valley above Hollin, where people dug for copper and lead for over 300 years. The path climbs along the old miners' track, past ruined workshops and the mouths of mine tunnels, to a high lake that fed the mine's water wheel. Boards along the way tell the story of the mine.

- **Start:** the bus stop at Hollin village green, the same as the Grey Tarn Circuit. Bus 41 from the city centre bus station takes about 50 minutes.
- **Distance:** 13.4 km, a loop.
- **Height gained:** 610 m.
- **Time:** about 5 hours.
- **Grade:** Blue.
- **Water:** the Hollin inn at the start fills bottles for walkers for free. The streams near the mine carry metal from the old workings, so do not drink from them, even with a filter.
- **Camping:** not allowed anywhere near the mine, because the ground can sink above the old tunnels.
- **Watch out:** never go into a tunnel, even a short way. The air inside can be bad, and the old wooden supports can give way. Bats sleep in the tunnels in winter, and the upper part of the trail is closed from November to March to leave them in peace.
- **Nearest shop:** City centre, near the bus station.

A free leaflet with a map of the old workings and the story of the miners' families is on the bar of the Hollin inn.

## Aldmoor Snowshoe Loop

Our only winter route. When snow covers the plateau, this loop near Aldmoor takes you across open, gently rolling ground and through snowy pine woods, always below the steep slopes. It is a good first day on snowshoes, and it ends at a warm café at the bus stop.

- **Start:** the Aldmoor bus stop. On Saturdays and Sundays from December to March, the winter bus 95 leaves from the stop in front of the Airport shop at 8:00 and takes 1 hour and 15 minutes.
- **Distance:** 6.1 km, a loop.
- **Height gained:** 260 m.
- **Time:** about 3 hours on snowshoes.
- **Grade:** Blue in winter. Without snow, it is a Green walk, but it is boggy.
- **Water:** the café at the bus stop is the only place. Bring a flask of hot drink; cold water from a bottle cools you down.
- **Camping:** not allowed on this loop. If you want to camp on snow, look at our winter camping course.
- **Watch out:** the loop stays on flat ground on purpose. Do not leave the marked poles to climb the slopes above, and when the avalanche bulletin gives danger level 4 or 5, do not walk even the loop. Days are short: start back by 15:00.
- **Nearest shop:** Airport, where the winter bus leaves.

Bring your own snowshoes. Use them with warm, waterproof boots, not with walking shoes.

## River Path to Elsby

A long but flat walk that starts in the middle of the city and follows the river upstream, away from the houses, through water meadows and willow woods, to the village of Elsby. You come back by train. It is a good walk to train for a long day, without any climbing.

- **Start:** our City centre shop on Market Street. Walk down the street to the river and turn left along the bank.
- **Distance:** 18.0 km, one way.
- **Height gained:** 150 m.
- **Time:** about 5 hours.
- **Grade:** Green.
- **Water:** you pass four villages, each with a café or a drinking fountain, so you need to carry only half a litre.
- **Camping:** not allowed along the river. Camping on the banks has been banned since the floods washed away tents some years ago.
- **Watch out:** the first 6 km are shared with cyclists. Keep to the right and listen for bells. In March, when the snow melts, the river can flood the path in the meadows; check the flood warning on the river office's website before you go.
- **Nearest shop:** City centre, at the start.

From Elsby Halt, the local train brings you back to the central station in 35 minutes. Elsby Halt is also where the Three Lakes Ridge starts, so you can join the two routes into a weekend.

## Osprey Point

A walk along the wild north shore of the lake to a rocky point where a pair of ospreys nest each summer. From a wooden hide at the end of the point, you can watch them fish. Bring binoculars if you have them, or borrow a pair from the bird club at the hide on weekends.

- **Start:** the bus stop at Gullet. Take bus 23 from the stop in front of the Lakeside shop in the other direction from Birchmere; the ride takes 15 minutes.
- **Distance:** 7.2 km, there and back.
- **Height gained:** 140 m.
- **Time:** about 2 and a half hours, plus time in the hide.
- **Grade:** Green.
- **Water:** none. There is a toilet but no tap at the Gullet bus stop.
- **Camping:** not allowed. The point is part of a bird reserve.
- **Watch out:** from 1 April to 31 July, while the birds nest, the last part of the path to the tip of the point is closed. You can still walk to the hide, which is far enough away not to disturb the birds. Dogs are not allowed past the gate at any time of year.
- **Nearest shop:** Lakeside, 15 minutes by bus.

Please talk quietly in the hide and do not use a camera flash.

## Rothwick Horseshoe

A round of three summits above Rothwick, in the shape of a horseshoe around a deep valley. It is easier than the Corran Ridge next door, with no hands needed, but long and steep, and it ends with a hard descent. You walk on grass and stony paths, with views of the Corran Teeth.

- **Start:** the Rothwick bus stop, at the end of bus line 41. The ride from the city centre bus station takes 1 hour and 10 minutes.
- **Distance:** 15.1 km, a loop.
- **Height gained:** 1,020 m.
- **Time:** about 6 hours.
- **Grade:** Red.
- **Water:** Rothwick Beck runs close to the path for the first 3 km. After that there is only the small tarn below the third summit; boil or filter it.
- **Camping:** wild camping is allowed next to Blackcomb Tarn, below the third summit. The ground is flat and dry.
- **Watch out:** the way down from the last summit goes straight down a slope of loose scree. It is fast when dry, but in rain or for tired legs it is dangerous. Then use the longer grassy path to the south, marked with red and blue stripes; it adds 2 km.
- **Nearest shop:** City centre, where bus 41 starts.

The Rothwick bus runs only every two hours on Sundays. Check the time of the last bus before you start.
