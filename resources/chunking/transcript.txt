Hi everyone, and welcome back to the Pinewood Outdoor channel. My name is Lena, I work in our test team, and today I want to show you how to pitch the Trail 2 tent. It's our most popular two-person tent, and once you know the steps, you can set it up in about ten minutes, even on your own. So let's start. First, choose a good place for your tent. You want flat ground, and you want to stay away from low points where water can collect when it rains. Look up as well, and don't pitch your tent under dead branches. If there's wind, try to find a place with some shelter, like a few bushes or a small hill. Okay, now open the bag. Inside you find the inner tent, the outer tent, which we call the fly, two poles, the pegs and the guy lines. Before you do anything else, count the pegs. There should be twelve. It's much easier to notice a missing peg at home than on a mountain at night, believe me. Now lay the inner tent flat on the ground, with the door facing away from the wind. Put a peg in each of the four corners, but don't push them in all the way yet. Just enough to keep the tent in place. Next, take the poles. They're connected with a cord inside, so they almost build themselves. Just let the sections click together, and check that every joint is closed. An open joint is the most common reason for a broken pole, so take a second to look. The two poles cross in the middle and go into the small metal rings at the corners. You'll feel the tent lift up as you put the second end in. Then clip the inner tent to the poles. Start at the bottom and work your way up to the top. Don't pull too hard, the clips go on easily. Now the inner tent is standing, and you can push the pegs in properly. Put them in at an angle of about forty-five degrees, with the top leaning away from the tent. That way they hold much better when the wind pulls on the tent. If the ground is very hard, you can use a stone to push them in, but please don't hit them hard with a hammer, because they can bend. Okay, next step, the fly. Throw it over the tent, and make sure the door of the fly is above the door of the inner tent. There's a small colour mark on the fly and on the inner tent, so just match the colours. Attach the fly to the corners with the buckles, and then pull the straps until the fly is tight. It should not touch the inner tent anywhere. If it touches, water from the outside can come through to the inside, especially when it rains for a long time. Now the guy lines. A lot of people skip them when the weather is nice, but I really recommend that you always use them. They keep the tent stable, they stop the fly from flapping in the wind, and they also help with condensation, because air can move between the two layers. Pull each line out straight from the seam, and peg it about one metre from the tent. Then tighten the small slider until the line is firm. And that's it, your tent is ready. Let me give you a few more tips before we finish. When you pack the tent in the morning, always try to let it dry first. If you have to pack it wet, take it out and dry it as soon as you get home, otherwise mould can grow on it. Mould is not covered by the warranty, so this one is important. Keep the poles and the pegs in their own small bags, so they don't make holes in the fabric. And close all the zips before you fold the tent, because that makes it much easier to fold and protects the zips. If something does break on a trip, every Trail 2 comes with a small repair kit in the bag. There's a pole sleeve for a broken pole section and a few patches for holes.
